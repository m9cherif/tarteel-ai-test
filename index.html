What I do

Most analysis fails long before the model does — it fails when nobody agreed on the question. I start there: what decision is waiting on this number, and who has to act on it.

From there it's the ordinary craft. Get the data out of the database, make it trustworthy, and put it somewhere a decision-maker will actually look — a Power BI dashboard, a clean Excel model, a Python notebook when the question needs more than a pivot table.

The commercial side isn't a separate skill for me. Having worked in business development and sales, I know what it's like to be the person the report is written for.

Territory & Pipeline Forecaster

Sales leaders were being handed a single number for the quarter — precise, confident, and wrong. I built a forecaster that answers the question they were actually asking: are we going to make it?

A calibrated model scores every open deal, a Monte Carlo simulation turns those into a distribution of quarter-end bookings, and a walk-forward backtest reports how wrong it has been before — 19% median error, with 83% of quarters landing inside its stated 80% band. It also says where next quarter's selling hours are worth most, which turned out to be a smaller lever than expected: the concentration sits in a handful of coin-flip deals, not in how territories are covered.
